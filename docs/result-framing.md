# Milestone 5: Research Result Framing

## Purpose

This document defines what Melbourne Urban Pulse can credibly present as a research result before narrative or visual design begins. It uses 16 deliberately selected manual-review candidates and their 64 reviewed evidence links. This is a purposive case set, not a representative sample of all 425 Pulses, so it cannot support annual prevalence claims.

## Result thesis

Known urban conditions do not map to one predictable pedestrian outcome. In the reviewed cases, observed deviations differ by temporal phase, network extent, duration, and combination of overlapping contexts. Reviewed evidence can establish that a condition coincided with a Pulse, but it does not isolate the condition as the cause.

The result is therefore not that holidays, rain, or events affect pedestrian activity. It is that the same high-level context can appear as different observable signatures, and that some signatures remain ambiguous or unresolved after evidence review.

## Recommended narrative cases

### 1. One date, two city rhythms

Pair the two New Year candidates. The complete 12-sensor network was above baseline from midnight to 05:00 and below baseline from 06:00 to 10:00. Event, crowd, transport, closure, holiday, and business evidence overlap different parts of the sequence. The useful result is the temporal phase reversal, not the discovery that New Year's Day is unusual.

Permitted claim:

> Melbourne's reviewed New Year sequence changed from a six-hour network-wide above-baseline Pulse to a five-hour network-wide below-baseline Pulse as the available context shifted from event dispersal to the public-holiday morning.

Do not claim that the event or holiday caused either Pulse.

### 2. Rain does not have one temporal footprint

Compare 6 January with 2 July. Both are reviewed below-baseline wet-weather cases, but the first covers 9 sensors for 3 hours while the second covers 11 sensors for 9 hours. The January window also overlaps school holidays and an external destination event; the July window is dominated by sustained cold and wet context.

Permitted claim:

> Reviewed wet-weather cases appeared with different network reach and duration, showing why a weather label alone is not a sufficient result.

Do not infer a rainfall-response curve from two purposively selected cases.

### 3. Known events leave different observable forms

Compare the Melbourne Marathon Pulse with the 17 December evening Pulse. Both are above baseline and have reviewed event evidence, but the marathon appears as a one-hour, 9-sensor early-morning Pulse, while the graduation and Christmas context overlaps an eight-hour, 8-sensor evening Pulse.

Permitted claim:

> Event-linked reviewed Pulses can differ substantially in temporal form even when both are broad above-baseline signals.

Do not compare event size or effectiveness: the project does not have attendance-normalised effects or a causal counterfactual.

### 4. The system must preserve what it cannot explain

Use the isolated QVM-Therry Street South episode as the boundary case. It is an eight-hour above-baseline episode at one sensor. Manual research found no matching regular market or night event, and the only evidence record is low confidence with an indeterminate expected direction.

Permitted claim:

> The reviewed workflow retains unresolved local signals instead of forcing a city event or weather explanation onto them.

Do not describe the negative search as proof that no local cause existed.

## Alternate civic case

The paired ANZAC candidates are a strong visual alternate: a network-wide above-baseline pre-dawn window is followed by a network-wide below-baseline wet afternoon. Use this instead of one core case if the final narrative needs a more Melbourne-specific civic sequence. Its interpretation must remain multi-factor and non-causal.

## Narrative structure

1. Establish regular rhythm and the meaning of above/below baseline.
2. Show the New Year phase reversal: one calendar label is not one urban response.
3. Compare weather and event forms, moving from named context to observable spatial-temporal signatures.
4. End with the unresolved QVM episode, making uncertainty part of the result.
5. Invite the reader into the annual Explorer to inspect the wider dataset.

## Claim boundary

The case study may say `coincided with`, `overlapped with`, `occurred during`, `consistent with`, or `plausibly associated with`. It must not say `caused`, `due to`, `because of`, `explained by`, or `attributed to`.

`verified_overlap` means the original researcher confirmed the evidence link. It does not convert an observational case into a causal result. No ranking, annual prevalence estimate, Urban Pulse Index, or generated causal explanation is supported by this milestone.

## Approval gate

Milestone 5 passes when the researcher approves:

- the result thesis;
- the four recommended narrative cases or an explicitly chosen alternate;
- the permitted claims and limitations;
- the narrative order that the full-site visual design must support.
